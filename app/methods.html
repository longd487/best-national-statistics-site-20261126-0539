Methods & sources

Where every number comes from.

StatAtlas reproduces published official statistics exactly as released. Nothing on this site is estimated, modelled or interpolated by us. Each series below names its source, its vintage and the page that uses it.

Methods and caveats

Annual averages

Unemployment, participation and CPI figures are annual averages of the monthly series, as published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. They are not December values.

Current dollars vs chained dollars

Nominal GDP is in current dollars; real GDP growth is the change in chained (2017) dollars. The two are not interchangeable, and the gap between them is the change in prices.

Vintages and revisions

Official statistics are revised. GDP figures follow the BEA’s 2026 annual update; population figures follow Census Vintage 2025. A figure quoted from an older vintage may differ slightly. Where two official sources publish slightly different values for the same concept (for example, the BEA and World Bank vintages of GDP), this site uses one vintage consistently within each series and says which.

Different surveys, different answers

The poverty rate is 10.2% on the American Community Survey’s 2020–2024 measure and 11.1% on the Current Population Survey’s 2023 measure. Both are correct for their own survey; they are not the same estimate, and this site labels which is which.

Rounding

Figures are shown rounded to the precision in the source. Totals may not sum exactly because of rounding — for example, goods and services exports ($2,083.8bn + $1,107.4bn) sum to $3,191.2bn against the published total of $3,191.6bn.

About this site

StatAtlas is an independent demonstration site built for the make.fun competition “Make the best national statistics site”. It is not affiliated with the U.S. government, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Census Bureau, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, or any other agency.

The design, text and charts are original. The site is a set of static files: it runs in a browser from its own files, makes no network requests, and loads no external fonts, scripts or images. All data lives in js/data.js, the single source of truth for every figure on every page; charts are drawn in the browser from that file.

Built with Node.js 22 and the standard library only. Run node main.ts from the repository root to verify the site: it checks that every page exists, that no page references anything outside the site, and that the screenshots are present.

Figures are reproduced as published by the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the U.S. Census Bureau, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the U.S. Department of the Treasury, the National Center for Health Statistics, the World Bank and UNDP. Data last checked 26 November 2026.
